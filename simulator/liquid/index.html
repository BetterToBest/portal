Liquid delegation: caps and cycles

Some voters hand their vote to someone they trust, and that person may hand it on. The paper proposes a cap so no delegate speaks for more than 1% of the population. This model shows what the cap and different cycle rules do to voting weight, and how often they change a close result.

Illustrative model, not a prediction. Each voter prefers A or B. A chosen share delegate instead of voting; each picks a delegate who may also be delegating, so chains form. A delegate's vote follows the delegate's own preference, so weight concentrates but expertise is not modeled. When a delegate is already at the cap, which delegators are turned away is decided by a random draw (a seeded shuffle), and they vote directly. Delegation is not issue-specific and does not expire, unlike the paper's proposal (section 4.4.2), so this tests only the cap and the loop rules. Outcome "differs" includes ties.
