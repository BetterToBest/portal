# A small advisory vote: a brief for community groups (draft v0.1)

Status: **draft for review.** It answers issue #14 and says only what the [pilot design](pilot-design.md) says. That design has not yet been reviewed in public, so everything here may change. The Citizens Internet Portal (CIP) is a research proposal, not a live voting system. No group has been approached, no dates are promised, and nothing here is an ethics approval or legal advice. Numbers are placeholders.

## What it would be

A small vote, run by a group that chooses to take part, on a question the group picks. It is **advisory**: the result binds no one. It would show what the people who took part said, and nothing more. The group is not the subject of a test. Together with the project, it would be checking whether the process is safe, understandable and verifiable.

## What it would not be

- A claim that CIP works, or would work at national scale.
- A way to settle a question the group is already fighting over. The first question should be low-stakes.
- A shortcut. The pilot would be the last step, after the software has met the checks in the [test plan](phase-3-test-plan.md). Today the ballot and identity parts are not built, so a vote could not run yet.

## What the group would decide

- Whether to take part, under its own written rules, and who on its side is responsible. It could leave at any time without giving a reason.
- The question, and who is eligible. The project would not decide who counts.

## What each person would be told and could do

- Before anything is made for them: what the software is, that it is a test, that the vote is advisory, what data exists and for how long.
- Saying no costs nothing. No reward depends on taking part or on how someone votes.
- A person could withdraw before casting a ballot. After that, nobody could pick out and remove one ballot, because ballots are not linked to people. This would be stated up front.

## Your data

Nothing would be collected or sold that tracks what people do, and nothing would link a person to how they voted. Names and contact details would never go into the public record. Any eligibility list or consent record would be kept by the group, apart from keys and ballots, and deleted soon after the vote (placeholder: 30 days).

## Who could take part

There would be more than one way in: a screen reader, a keyboard alone, a small screen, an older or borrowed device. Wording would be plain and in the group's languages, with no countdowns and no remote help from the project. Anyone who could not use the software could still give their view the usual way, and the report would say who was left out.

## What would be published

A report, whatever the result. It would say the vote is advisory and what it does not show, give the question as shown, the number who took part and the count for each option, list problems found, and say who could not take part. The group could read it first to correct errors of fact and add a reply, but could not remove findings. If fewer than about 20 people took part (placeholder), the report would cover the process and not the vote.

## Who could stop it

Any person, before casting a ballot. The group, at any time. The node operator, the independent observer and the project, for a safety, integrity or data problem. Each stop would be public. Nobody could change the rules once voting opened.
