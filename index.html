Democracy Infrastructure
Aspiring to achieve immunity from capture.

The Citizens Internet Portal (CIP) is a proposed open-source platform for voting, budgeting and oversight, designed so that corrupting it would cost far more than it could pay back.

Why reform keeps failing.

Concentrated power attracts corruption. Spreading power out helps, until corruptors target the spreading mechanisms themselves.

Has anyone tried to convince the corruptors to cease corrupting? The paper's answer: don't ask. Design the system so corruption cannot succeed.

The paper calls this goal structural incorruptibility. It reviews why ethics rules, transparency and prosecution fail inside a captured system, and points to post-Citizens United spending growth as the case study: political spending rose from $3.1 billion in the 2008 cycle to $14.4 billion in 2020.

The Judicial Guard.

U.S. currency integrity is protected by the Secret Service. The paper proposes a similar protective enforcement agency for democratic participation.

Many, not few

500 to 2,000+ independent guardians instead of a council that could be bought. Nobody can order a guardian to end an investigation.

Many paths to prosecution

Federal, state and local prosecutors, special prosecutors, citizen grand juries and more. One honest prosecutor is enough to pursue a case.

Public scorecard

Every criminal case referral and declination is published on CIP. A high decline rate triggers automatic escalation.

One layer of a larger system.

CIP is the democratic layer of the Compassionism Framework, a set of connected architectures. Here is each system in brief, then how CIP connects to it.

CCO: Creative Currency Octaves

A dual-currency system. Citizens can opt in to receive monthly-expiring Basic Units for essentials like food, housing and utilities, with unlimited earning potential through a conversion system run by creative collectives.

PTF: Public Trust Foundations

Not-for-profit and community-benefit organizations that provide foundational goods and services such as groceries, utilities and restaurants. Private businesses can also operate within the system's boundaries. They accept Basic Units for goods and services according to the local charter and oversight agencies.

PTH: Public Trust Housing

Community-owned housing held in public trust. Residents govern it collectively and hold individual equity accounts that grow with participation.

SZH: Social Zone Harmonization

A way to organize communities around shared values and lifestyles, such as family-safe zones or artist districts, while keeping local autonomy.

The proposed path.

  1. Foundation and pilot (years 1 to 3)

    Legal framework, first infrastructure, and pilots in 3 to 5 cities with advisory votes.

  2. Expansion (years 4 to 7)

    More cities and states, moving from advisory to binding referenda.

  3. National implementation (years 8 to 15)

    Federal authorization and integration with the legislative process.

  4. Maturation (years 16 to 25)

    Continuous improvement and international adoption.

Timelines and targets come from the paper and are proposals, not commitments. Because CIP can be built and tested as an open-source project, these timelines could be accelerated well beyond what the institutional phases assume.
